# M1 movement playground report

## What works

The local playground advances a tank in the shared simulation inside a Worker. WASD drives in world directions; the hull turns towards the requested direction and the separately drawn turret turns toward the mouse. Shift slows movement for silent running. Grass, road, mud and brush have their specified speed factors, while walls and world bounds block the circular hull. The camera follows with bounded aim lead. The HUD shows speed, terrain and when the silent speed cap has been reached. Losing focus neutralizes movement.

Run `npm ci` and `npm run dev`, then open http://127.0.0.1:5173/ and click the game. Use WASD, mouse and Shift. The server connection remains an M0 diagnostic; the movement playground is local, not an online game.

## Verification

`npm test` covers diagonal input, rotation limits, turning in place, braking, terrain and silent speed, wall sliding, and fixed simulation steps across different render schedules. The browser was checked manually for drawing, Worker startup, server connection and driving input. Playwright checks that focus loss neutralizes a held driving input. `npm run build` and `npm run validate:map` also pass.

## Status and limitations

M1 movement playground is **complete as an initial slice**. The owner tried it and reported that they liked it; this is early driving feedback, not a combat or stealth playtest. Terrain transitions are covered headlessly; collision needs further edge-case testing as the game develops. The client currently sends input every render frame; presentation interpolates Worker snapshots. Sound suppression, stealth, shooting, combat, multiplayer movement, prediction, and pause are not implemented. Shapes and terrain colors are placeholders. No human playtest gate has been reached.

The foundation and performance reference are recorded in the [M0 report](m0.md). No balance tuning has been changed from the specification.

## Inertia tuning — November 4, 2026

Owner feedback requested more inertia and acceleration/deceleration curves. Replaced constant-rate speed changes with smoothstep S-curves: acceleration 0.6 seconds (18 ticks), braking 0.35 seconds rounded up to 11 ticks (0.367 seconds). Grass coasting from full speed is roughly 30 px. Speed ramps retain progress and continuity when turning or charge continuously changes the requested cap. Hull/turret rates remain unchanged. This supersedes the specification's initial 0.25-second acceleration and 0.15-second stop defaults; assess responsiveness in the next owner playtest.

TDD: new curve/coasting assertions failed against constant acceleration, then passed after implementing curves. A test for continuous charge slowdown exposed excessive lag from restarting a curve every tick; retargeting the active curve fixed it. All 16 headless tests and the build pass; browser controls were checked locally. This changes forward speed response, not lateral sliding or physical recoil.


## Presentation smoothing — November 4, 2026

Owner reported judder despite improved movement curves. Added presentation-only interpolation for hull position/rotation, world-relative turret rotation, charge ring and existing shell positions. The camera follows the interpolated tank. The authority remains at 30 Hz; rendering samples positions each frame with one simulation tick (about 33 ms) of visual delay. New shells appear at their first authorized position and removed shells disappear immediately, without extrapolation beyond a confirmed impact. Snapshots after a 250 ms gap reset interpolation; duplicate/older ticks are ignored.

TDD: the latest-state baseline failed intermediate-position, shortest-angle and stale-snapshot tests. The interpolator made all tests pass. Local results: 20 unit tests, five browser checks and production build pass. CI was not triggered. Human assessment of smoothness and measured frame pacing remain pending; this is not a claim of measured 60 FPS. A later prediction implementation can reduce the local visual delay.

## Owner-requested drift experiment — November 4, 2026

Combat playtest feedback: hull-alignment restrictions felt like the controls were fighting the player. The owner requested faster direction changes and substantially more sliding inertia. This explicitly supersedes the original hull-only/alignment-taper contract for the current experiment; it adds no ice terrain.

Driving now steers toward the requested world direction immediately while hull art turns independently at 660 degrees/second (180 degrees in about 0.27 seconds). World vx/vy persist through turns. Steering exponentially approaches the terrain/silent/charge-adjusted desired velocity with a 0.2-second response constant. Releasing input uses a weaker 0.55-second drag constant, producing approximately 95 px of coasting from full grass speed and a gradual stop. Countersteering redirects momentum faster than passive drag. Walls cancel only the blocked velocity component so tanks can slide tangentially; respawn/reset clears velocity. Turret traverse and top-speed factors are preserved. Both human and bots use these same rules.

TDD: updated immediate-input, drift/coast and countersteering tests failed against the previous movement model, then passed with vector velocity. Collision/sliding, diagonal normalization, terrain/charge caps, render-rate independence and respawn velocity reset are covered. Removed the superseded scalar ramp state. Local results: 54 unit tests, 13 browser checks and the build pass. The 100-match idle-human diagnostic completed all seeds, with a maximum five shells, 2,977 deaths and a maximum 0.3-second measured bot stop away from the objective. Red won all runs against the idle human; this remains a smoke test, not balance evidence. No CI run.

This is an owner-requested S1 playtest revision, not a passed new feel gate. Assess whether drifting adds enjoyable recovery/countersteering without making precise positioning frustrating.
