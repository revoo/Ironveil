## World and rules

Relay Yard is authored as a Tiled-compatible JSON map with a small original SVG terrain atlas in shared/content/maps/. It contains terrain, collision/occlusion cover, layout bounds, team spawns, ordered objectives and landmarks. The full 2400 × 1600 arrangement and explicitly authored 1600 × 960 small arrangement normalize from that source. Four-tank solo uses the small layout. Radio Tower, Depot and Broken Bridge appear in the world and minimap. Broken Bridge is cover/scenery, not water. Full-layout normalization and route validation are delivered; full-layout gameplay balance has not been playtested.

Normalization rejects unsupported geometry, layers/terrain/property data, duplicate object IDs, invalid bounds, missing/invalid spawn and objective arrangements, and insufficient hull clearance. Build validation checks spawn-to-site reachability in both layouts. A small-map test checks at least three reachable cardinal approach points per site. These checks do not establish good encounter pacing, cover balance or independent tactical route quality; those need playtest evidence.

Living teammates share the union of 440 px wall/smoke-clear vision. Brush occupants are concealed beyond 110 px unless firing reveal is active for 36 ticks (1.2 seconds). Firing reveal never bypasses hard cover, smoke or range. Charging creates sound, not brush reveal. Losing sight removes the live sprite and motion history, leaving a frozen, aging last-known X for approximately three seconds. Map geometry is known from the beginning; unsighted ground is dimmed rather than unexplored. Ground shading refreshes at 10 Hz; entity authorization is evaluated on each observation, independently of camera culling.

Smoke: 100 px radius immediately, 150 ticks (five seconds), 540-tick (18-second) cooldown. It blocks rays in both directions, including rays originating inside a cloud, and does not block shells. It cancels charge and applies at least eight recovery ticks without shortening a longer weapon recovery. It does not erase tracks, suppress sound or self-damage. Clouds have no visual fade before their gameplay lifetime ends. Own tank and interface remain visible inside smoke. Death/respawn restores the ability; dead/paused/finished actors cannot deploy it.

Engine sounds use about 4 Hz bounded emissions while moving (every eight ticks); terrain modifies engine radius only: grass/road 300 px, mud ×1.25, brush ×0.75. Shift suppresses engine emissions only once actual speed reaches the terrain-adjusted silent cap. Charge, shot and smoke radii are 350/700/200 px. Sound packets contain only opaque cue ID, sound kind, fixed eight-sector direction, near/far band and expiration. They contain no source coordinates, tank ID or exact distance. Audio panning and edge arcs use those same sectors/bands, including with sound muted. They are listener-specific; dead listeners do not hear via an ally's location. Visible-source cues may also use this deliberately coarse representation. No hidden-position minimap dots are sent.

Mud leaves a stamp roughly each 32 px traveled, at most six per tank per second, including during silent running. Stamps last 360 ticks (12 seconds), use three coarse fade stages, and contain position, opaque ID, canonical tread axis and known expiration. Sixteen axial orientation bins are identical for opposite directions; no owner, team, route ID or creation timestamp is transmitted. A negative-remainder regression caught and fixed packet values that could distinguish opposite travel despite identical rendering. A stamp is first observed only when living team vision sees its ground through walls/smoke. Brush affects vehicles, not ground evidence. Observed stamps are historical and remain unchanged until known expiration; hidden new stamps are not added. Authority and each team's retained memory are capped at 640, expiring before oldest eviction. Client observations remain bounded to that memory.

## Bots and presentation

The same authorized observation interface supplies bots and player rendering. Bots now use smoke to reposition at low health, approach nearby evidence quietly, and briefly investigate stale sightings, anonymous stamps or coarse sound sectors. Evidence gives a historical or coarse goal, never a hidden live transform or track-owner association. Acquisition and firing still require a currently authorized enemy and a wall-clear firing line; losing contact cancels charging. Investigation and repositioning are bounded before returning to the relay.

A new map exposed a blocked formation offset near Radio Tower: A* correctly rejected the target but the bot could linger. The regression failed before the fix; bots now fall back to the reachable relay center. Waypoint steering, terrain costs, hull inflation, seeded aim error and shared combat values remain intact.

Player presentation adds dimmed ground, clear smoke circles, muddy tread evidence, stale X markers, sector/band sound labels and arcs, explicit smoke cooldown, and factual quiet/firing-reveal indicators. There is no omniscient “enemy sees you” indicator. Visible projectiles carry only their authorized current state, without shooter identity or original muzzle location. Effects/audio remain procedural placeholders. The approved drift controls were not retuned.

## Verification and evidence

Local results: **70 unit tests, 14 browser checks, map/route validation and production build pass**. Browser tests include real Worker versus Node early-step agreement and repeatable Worker replay, pause/restart/results, sound unlock/mute, held-fire safety, and Q smoke/cooldown freezing. The Node JSON importer initially failed in the Playwright runner; explicit JSON import attributes fixed compatibility with Node 20 as well as Vite/Worker builds.

Tests were written before brush/smoke perception, stale memory, tracks, coarse sounds, map normalization, bot evidence/smoke choices and the Q control integration. Initial failures demonstrated the missing behavior; green implementation followed. Additional invariants cover smoke passing shells, evidence bounds, braking into silence, charge without brush reveal, longer recovery preservation, and hidden-shooter projectile DTOs. Some initial absent-data assertions failed on missing DTO fields rather than gameplay outcomes; they are contract checks, not independent behavioral evidence.

The final 100 seeded Relay Yard matches with an idle human all terminated. Red won all 100, with 1,785 deaths, at most five active shells, and a longest measured bot stop away from a relay of 2.23 seconds. These are diagnostic observations, not difficulty/balance claims or exhaustive stuck-recovery proof. The full source and small layout are validated; no multiplayer load or frame-time target is claimed.

A visual inspection confirmed the local map, fog, landmarks, minimap and HUD. Smoke behavior, timing and controls also pass automated checks. Packet-shape tests inspect evidence and projectile DTOs; online join/reconnect/state-view filtering is still deferred, not validated by solo tests. No GitHub CI run was triggered for this milestone; local verification is complete.

## Playtest gate and limits

For the complete milestone, look for concrete encounters: a meaningful tap-versus-charge choice; breaking contact and returning from another approach; quiet movement or mud evidence changing a decision; and an understandable ambush with room to respond. Report tedious searching, dominant maximum charge, confusing visibility, bots that seem unfair, ignored stealth, or strong positions without useful alternatives. Evaluate solo fun and readability, not multiplayer balance. Record passed or revise with examples; the original several-human-match Gate B remains unperformed and must not be inferred from this solo playtest.

Difficulty presets, final art/audio mix, tutorial/settings/accessibility polish and atomic offline caching are S3 work. Smoke uses Q as the documented default; remapping is later. Brush is the vegetation mechanic; forest-depth occlusion, ice terrain, new weapons and other deferred mechanics were not added. The map is a gray box and may need route/pacing changes after playtests. Node/Chromium can diverge over long deterministic combat trajectories as previously documented. Existing dependency advisories and the large Phaser bundle warning remain; this is not a public deployment package. Protocol/content versions are 3.
